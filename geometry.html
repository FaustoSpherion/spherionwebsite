Spherion Geometry

The sphere as primitive.

Spherion Geometry begins with a reversal: the whole spherical field is primary, and local description appears through zones, overlaps, refinement, adjacency, and observer-relative motion.

Six overlapping zones

The original Spherion intuition divides the sphere through six interpenetrating hemispherical fields: up/down, left/right, and front/back. Their overlaps create shared subzones and allow ambiguity to be represented structurally.

Refinement

Zones may be recursively subdivided to increase resolution. Precision is therefore not assumed at the beginning; it is generated by refinement.

Adjacency

At each refinement level, zones can be related by an adjacency graph. Neighborhood is defined structurally before being reduced to numerical distance.

Layering

Internal layers introduce radial depth. Motion can be lateral, radial, or mixed, depending on how a path moves across shells and refinement levels.

Formal orientation

Useful website-level statements

Zone membership. A local region may be represented as a member of one or more overlapping zones:

z∈Zℓ

Neighborhood. A zone has a set of immediate neighbors in an adjacency graph:

Nℓ(z)={q∈Zℓ:q∼z}

Time as derived index. In the current Spherion direction, time is not primitive; it appears where refinement coherence becomes meaningful.

Motion in Spherion

Linear

A path can be linear relative to an observer when it remains centered within that observer’s reference frame and does not generate a rotational displacement.

Clockwise / Counterclockwise

The same global movement may appear clockwise to one observer and counterclockwise to another. Motion classification is therefore frame-sensitive.

Ambiguous

When path evidence crosses overlapping zones or insufficient refinement is available, Spherion can preserve ambiguity rather than forcing premature classification.
