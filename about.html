About Spherion

From early Spherion Metrics to a broader geometric and operational framework.

Spherion was created and developed by Fausto Machado Freire, also published as Favst McFrey. The project began as an attempt to rethink spatial representation through the sphere, then evolved into a larger framework involving geometry, algebra, probability, motion, and computation.

Origin

The earliest formulation emphasized Spherion Metrics: a way of replacing ordinary coordinate dependence with spherical subdivision, overlapping hemispheres, and hierarchical precision. That remains part of the historical foundation, but it is no longer the whole project.

The current framework is better described through three layers: Spherion Geometry, Spherion Algebra, and the Spherion Operational System kernel.

The present definition

Spherion is a sphere-first research framework in which structure precedes measurement, zones precede coordinates, uncertainty is encoded through overlapping inclusion, and motion is understood as transformation across refinement, adjacency, and observer frames.

How to read the project

Not merely a variation of Euclidean geometry.

Conceptual

Spherion asks what happens if geometry does not begin with the point, line, plane, or coordinate grid, but with a structured spherical whole.

Mathematical

It develops formal objects such as zones, refinement paths, adjacency graphs, layered neighborhoods, and probabilistic inclusion.

Computational

It points toward algorithms that operate on discrete spherical tokens, uncertainty weights, and deterministic refinement/collapse rules.

Creator and invitation

The purpose of this site is not only to explain Spherion to the public, but also to invite rigorous review. Mathematicians, physicists, computer scientists, engineers, educators, and independent researchers are welcome to examine the definitions, improve the notation, test the algorithms, and challenge the assumptions.
